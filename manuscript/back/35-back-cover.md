<div class="backcover-page">
  <div class="backcover-hook">One book knew money. The other knew
  why money knows some people. They were written sixty years apart,
  and they fit like two halves of one machine.</div>

  <p>In 1880, the most famous showman alive wrote the habits: avoid
  debt, buy the best tools, hammer one nail, never sign without
  security, keep your name where the public can find you, and stay
  honest when honesty costs. In 1910, a quiet Midwesterner wrote what
  runs underneath the habits: the exact way of thinking that reliably
  produces wealth, for anyone, starting anywhere.</p>

  <p><span class="backcover-tag">The Gilded Code</span> welds the two
  books into twenty-two laws and strips a century of mysticism and
  hype off both. Each law is a short chapter in plain modern voice,
  ending with two lines that do the work: a <em>Do this now</em> you
  can run in ten minutes, and a <em>Track it</em> prompt that turns
  the law into your own written record. The back matter hands you a
  tracker, a thirty-day planner, and the whole code on one page.</p>

  <p>No lottery tickets. No metaphysics. No promises the source books
  never made. Just the pattern both men lived: want more of life, see
  it clearly first, do it daily, and handle every person and every
  dollar honestly.</p>

  <p class="backcover-laws">Twenty-two laws. Thirty days. Four laps.
  Start tonight with law one.</p>

  <p class="backcover-byline"><strong>Kael Varnow</strong> writes the
  missing instruction manuals for ideas that already worked.</p>
</div>
