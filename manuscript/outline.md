## The parts

**Part I — The Price of the Mind (laws 1–8, Wattles-sourced):** what has
to be true in a reader's head before habits work.

**Part II — The Code in Practice (laws 9–18, Barnum-sourced):** the daily
conduct of money.

**Part III — Living the Code (laws 19–22, synthesis):** the two books'
later material interwoven — advancement, business fit, integrity, and the
whole code restated.

## The 22 laws

1. **The Right to Be Rich** — wanting wealth is the wish for a bigger life, and poverty is no virtue. *Opener: "Before any other step: forgive yourself for wanting."* Anecdote kept: Wattles' poverty-as-failure-of-living argument, reframed.
2. **Getting Rich Is Not Luck — It Is Law** — cause and effect, not accident: anyone who does things in a certain way gets rich; talent, location, capital are minor variables. *Opener: "Riches obey arithmetic, not astrology."*
3. **Opportunity Is Not Monopolized** — you are not late; doors close, windows open; scarcity talk is the mind's habit, not the world's state.
4. **Think Like an Owner** — thought as the seed of circumstance; life presses for fuller expression; the difference between bargaining for wage and building for ownership. Wattles' "formless stuff" reframed plainly: mind sets price on effort.
5. **See It Before You Have It** — the generous, clear picture of the life you want; vagueness is the enemy; desire sharpened into a definite image. *Do this now: write the exact picture.*
6. **The Gratitude Switch** — why gratitude welds attention to abundance and not to lack; daily discipline, not sentiment.
7. **Mind Your Own Mind** — hold your own thought; do not fight visible conditions or try to bend other people; the will is for keeping your attention on your picture, never for coercing.
8. **Do All That Can Be Done Today** — acting on your best present belief; no misdirected tomorrow-daydreaming substitute for today's work; quiet, full, efficient action.
9. **Do Not Mistake Your Vocation** — pick work consonant with your nature; parents, peers, pay can push you into the wrong skin; exception note from Barnum: taste can be trained.
10. **Put Yourself Where the Demand Is** — the story Barnum tells of the hotel-keeper's genius misplaced; right trade, wrong town = slow death.
11. **Debt Bites Slow and Holds Hard** — the young man "succeeded" for the new suit; wife and household dragged behind the debt-wagon; sixty-day habit of paying as you go.  Barnum's anecdote of the debtor's family kept, shortened.
12. **Whatever Is Worth Doing, Take Your Whole Self** — perseverance plus whole-mindedness; "no stone unturned"; Crockett's "be sure you are right, then go ahead" kept; late and early work; detest waiting.
13. **The Best Tools Win — Including People** — get and keep the best employees and instruments; the dull-saw cost (Barnum's woodchoppers story as told in Use the Best Tools/whatever-you-do sections); under-buying is over-paying.
14. **Serve Your Trade, Not Your Vanity** — don't get above your business; learn something useful for the rainy day; the pianoforte/carpenter framing Barnum uses, modernized to portable skills.
15. **Hope Much, Bet Little** — hope predominates but cash follows proof: the visionary-changer, the excursion analogy, counting chickens; hammer one nail.
16. **A Time and a Place for Everything** — system multiplies power: the man with time for everything; also Barnum's "read the newspapers" rendered as staying current/informed daily.
17. **Side Bets Sink Ships** — outside operations and indorsement-without-security; the foundering merchant stories kept in modern dress.
18. **Make the Public Look Your Way** — advertising honestly; Genin's $225 hat ticket and the "don't read the other side" poster story; politeness as the best capital; genuine goods, generous treatment.
19. **Leave Everyone a Little Better** — the impression of increase and the advancing man (W) joined to charity and silence about your deals (B18, B19); give more in use-value; don't blab your profits, hopes, or intentions.
20. **Get Into the Right Business** — Wattles' test (use for the world more than you take), don't rush from a job not yet outgrown; talent for the thing plus tools available; retrainability argument.
21. **Integrity Is the Fortune That Cannot Be Lost** — the miser's "get money if you must steal it" advice as the essence of stupidity; trust as capital; the law never counts an honest fortune lost. W XVI cautions: don't argue with appearances.
22. **The Whole Code in One Hand** — Wattles' summary chapter restated in the code's own vocabulary; the 22 laws in one page; what to do for the next 30 days.
