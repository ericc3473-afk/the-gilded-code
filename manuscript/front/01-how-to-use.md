# How to Use This Book

This is not a book you read once. It is a machine you come back to.

Twenty-two laws, in three parts. Part I rebuilds the way you think about
money. Part II rebuilds what you actually do. Part III welds the two
together and shows you how to run the code for the rest of your life.

**Pass one: the full read.** Read the book straight through, one part at
a time. Don't stop to optimize your life on day two. Let the whole
pattern show up first. Each chapter ends with two lines worth more than
the rest of the page:

> **Do this now:** one physical action, usually under ten minutes.
>
> **Track it:** one thing to log in your journal or notes app. The book
> doesn't track for you; you keep the log. That's deliberate. The log is
> yours, and you can't lose a habit you're writing down.

**Pass two: the daily cycle.** After your full read, the book folds up
into ninety seconds a day:

1. Read today's **law:** one chapter title and its opener line. The
   openers alone are the whole code, compressed. (Back matter has all
   twenty-two on one page, ready to skim.)
2. Do its **Do this now:** or the version you logged last time.
3. Write your **Track it** line.

Run the cycle in order, law 1 through law 22. That is one complete lap,
roughly a month of days. Then run it again. The tracker in the back
matter holds four laps, and the thirty-day planner holds one. Print
those pages or copy the grid into your notebook; check off a law when
you have lived it for a full day, not when you have read it.

**The honest rules.**

- Don't skip pass one. People who skim self-help books and wonder why
  nothing changed did exactly that.
- Track everything. Reading these laws does nothing; rehearsing them
  daily rewires what you reach for at the decision moment.
- Two laws will sting. That's the point. The stinging ones are the ones
  about you.
- Four laps is the goal, not perfection. A law you keep four cycles beats
  a law you "get."

**What you will need.** A pen and a notebook, or a notes app you trust.
Ninety seconds a day. And permission to be exact with yourself, since
the whole design assumes the log is honest.

**When life interferes.** You will miss days. The rule is simple: the
cycle does not reset to the front of the book. Resume at the law you
left, half-finished counts, and the missed lap is absorbed by the next
one. A restart costs you nothing except the decision to restart, which
is the one cost the plan is built to avoid.

**If a law does not apply to you.** Twenty-two laws is more than any
one life needs on a Tuesday. Some laws will look irrelevant: you have
no debts, no employees, no business. Run the law anyway in its smallest
form. No debts to control means the law applies to the next debt you
could create. No employees to choose means you are still choosing
which clients, projects, and colleagues get your hours. Every law has a
personal-life form and a work form; each chapter says so where it
matters.
