P. T. Barnum knew money. Wallace Wattles knew why money knows some people. They never met, but their books fit like two halves of one machine.

In 1880, the most famous showman alive wrote the habits: avoid debt, buy the best tools, hammer one nail, never sign without security, keep your name where the public can find you, and stay honest when honesty costs. In 1910, a quiet Midwesterner wrote what runs underneath the habits: the exact way of thinking that reliably produces wealth, for anyone, starting anywhere.

The Gilded Code welds the two books into twenty-two laws and strips a century of mysticism and hype off both. Each law is a short chapter in plain modern voice, ending with two lines that do the work: a Do this now you can run in ten minutes, and a Track it prompt that turns the law into your own written record. The back matter hands you a tracker, a thirty-day planner, and the whole code on one page.

No lottery tickets. No metaphysics. No promises the two authors never made. Just the pattern both men lived: want more of life, see it clearly first, do it daily, and handle every person and every dollar honestly.

Twenty-two laws. Thirty days. Four laps. Start tonight with law one.

Condensed and rewritten from two public-domain classics: P. T. Barnum's The Art of Money Getting (1880) and Wallace D. Wattles' The Science of Getting Rich (1910).
